# v2.4 Amendment — Feasibility Re-Examination

**Status: APPROVED BY THE OWNER 2026-10-11 ("go"). Consumed the same day.**
It amends the ratified v2.4 proposal
(`2026-10-09-v2.4-roadmap-proposal.md`, `0b400dfa`). Where the two differ,
this document governs.

## In one paragraph

The owner asked two more things of v2.4: what else it should achieve beyond
a go/stop answer, and what we would need to judge where the project stands
overall. Re-examining the milestone found four ways it could mislead.

1. It could say "can't be done" because one locked setting is wrong.
2. It could say "go" on a result that a textbook formula, with no learning,
   would match.
3. It could fail an experiment for lacking the polishing optimizer that the
   spec itself calls essential.
4. It never looks past Stage 0, so a Stage-0 success could hide a dead end
   later.

It also lacked what any long project needs to judge progress: comparison
points, a fixed map of capability levels, a cost estimate, and a rule for
when to stop. This amendment adds those. It costs about one to two extra
days in Phase 59, plus overnight computing that runs only when its
pre-registered condition fires.

**One placement change from what was presented.** The hard-stage probes

work. They run after G2 passes, and can run overnight while Phase 61 is

unchanged.

### The two-Kepler formula (FEAS-07)

The baseline a first-year physics student could write, with no learning:

- bodies 1 and 2 form the inner pair by construction
  (`sampling_method.md:23`, `:47-50`); their relative orbit is a Kepler
  orbit with mass `m₁ + m₂`, from their initial relative position and
  velocity;
- body 3 follows a Kepler orbit about the inner pair's centre of mass, with
  mass `m₁ + m₂ + m₃`;
- positions are rebuilt in the centre-of-mass frame and scored by the same
  scorer as the network.

It ignores everything the third body does to the inner pair. A network that
cannot beat it on a training system has not learned the interaction, whatever
its tier.

### The nearest-neighbour copy (FEAS-07)

For each validation-panel system, the prediction is the Brutus trajectory of
the training system nearest in normalized IC13. It answers the owner's
"little data" worry directly. If copying scores well, 1,812 systems cover
Stage 0 densely and the network only needs to interpolate. If it scores
badly, the network must genuinely learn from sparse examples.

### The predictability envelope (FEAS-08)

For each stage, the survey reports how sharply the end of the window depends
on the starting conditions: the error growth `e^{T/τ_L}` from the recorded
Lyapunov time. It also reports the inner laps per window, the closest
approach, and the acceleration range. This shows, before anything is trained
beyond Stage 0, whether the later stages are reachable for a model learning
from about 2,000 systems per stage. In particular, it shows whether a
per-system `10⁻⁵` energy bar is plausible at Stages 4b and 6.

## The amended decision rule

It replaces the proposal's "Decision rule" section.

**Counting.** On a training system, a rung *counts* only when it is at
Tier ≥ 1 **and** its tier-defining position error is below the two-Kepler
formula's on that system. FEAS-01 defines the tier-defining position error
so that it covers the inner pair, for example the larger of the two forms.

**An arm's result.** Every single-system arm runs Adam under the budget rule,
then the L-BFGS polish. Both states are scored, and the arm's result is the
better of the two. Rung 3 and the control stay Adam-only (`JD-6` A,
`trainex-r4-ruling.md:107`), so their results are read as Adam-phase results.

**Gate G2.**
1. Rung 1 runs at the locked settings, with the polish and the budget rule.
   If it is below Tier 1 on 2 or more of 3, the G2 settings sweep runs.
   - The sweep tries at most four alternative representation configurations,
     with the grid fixed in FEAS-01 before any rung runs, data-only, on one
     failing system.
   - A configuration that reaches Tier 1 there is confirmed on a second
     failing system.
   - Being data-only, the sweep never touches `R_ref`.
2. **No rescue:** RETHINK-REPRESENTATION. Phases 61–63 are skipped, and Phase
   64 follows.
3. **Rescue:** owner checkpoint ④. A locked setting is the bottleneck, which
   is fixable. The owner decides whether to amend it formally (SCOPE-43) and
   continue the ladder on it, or to go to the verdict. The recommendation
   brought to the owner is to amend and continue.

**The verdict (FEAS-06):**

- **RETHINK-REPRESENTATION:** G2 fired and the sweep rescued nothing.
- **PIVOT-TRAINING:** G2 passed, but any of these holds:
  - Rung 2b counts on fewer than 2 of 3;
  - Rung 3 counts on fewer than 12 of 16;
  - the control's readout is not branch (a).

  The verdict names the part of the training method that failed. Where Rung 3
  fell short, the unreached L-BFGS stage is a named candidate cause.
- **GO:** all three hold:
  - Rung 2b counts on at least 2 of 3;
  - Rung 3 counts on at least 12 of 16;
  - the control's readout is branch (a).
- **Qualifier.** If Rung 1 itself, with the answer shown, does not beat the
  formula on at least 2 of 3, the verdict names the representation as the
  limit, whatever the branch.
- **Reported, not gating:**
  - Rung 2a;
  - the validation-panel scores beside the formula and the nearest-neighbour
    copy;
  - the hard-stage probes (FEAS-09);
  - the survey (FEAS-08);
  - the distance to `10⁻⁵`.

## The readiness ladder

The verdict places the project on this ladder, the map of what it can do.
Levels are capabilities. Levels 2 and 3 can be reached in either order. The
verdict reports every level reached, and the highest level with every level
below it also reached.

| Level | Capability | How it is shown | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Data and specs exist | — | reached |
| 1 | The training code runs | — | reached (not to spec) |
| 2 | The code provably matches the spec | CONF-10/11 pass, and a mechanical code-against-spec check passes | v2.4, Phase 61 |
| 3 | Learns one system | Rung 2b counts on at least 2 of 3 | v2.4, Phase 60 |
| 4 | Learns a small family | Rung 3 counts on at least 12 of 16 | v2.4, Phase 62 |
| 5 | Learns all of Stage 0 and predicts unseen Stage-0 systems | Full training; the validation panel at the charter's bar, beating the formula and the copy | next milestone |
| 6 | Passes the one-shot Stage-0 final exam | The preserved held-out evaluation, at the charter's bar | next milestone |
| 7 | Stages 1–2 (stable) | Charter bars | later |
| 8 | Stages 3–5 (chaotic) | Charter bars | later |
| 9 | Stage 6 (near-collisions) | Charter bars | later |

## The cost model (FEAS-06)

The verdict states:

- the machine hours v2.4 spent, per rung;
- the estimated cost to the next level and to the finish, on this Mac and on
  a rented GPU. The estimate uses the measured seconds per step and the
  accuracy curves, with every assumption stated, including the GPU speed-up,
  which this machine cannot measure.

**For scale only.** At generation 7's 12.3 seconds per step, the roughly
200,000 Adam steps the 2026 PINN study used for one orbit would take about a
month of nonstop computing. v2.4's curves replace that illustration with
measurements.

## The pass bar: what v2.4 decides and what it does not

- **The owner's lean.** Relax the `10⁻⁵` bar, decided later, with evidence.
  The published record supports it:
  - Breen et al. report about 1% energy error, spiking at close encounters,
    reaching about `10⁻⁵` only through a post-hoc projection, on a
    2-parameter family against our 13;
  - the 2026 PINN study reports 0.08–3% energy drift on single periodic
    orbits;
  - our own spec calls under 0.1% a success (`ctl_instruction.md:2057-2058`).
- **What v2.4 does.** It keeps Tier 3 in the scorecard as the distance to
  the locked bar, and decides nothing about the bar itself.
- **What the charter (FEAS-10) does.** It sets each stage's bar from the
  project's purpose and the comparison points. Every bar:
  - has a position criterion **and** an energy criterion, never energy
    alone, because a projection can fix energy without making the orbit any
    more correct;
  - is fixed before any held-out exam, never after a result is seen.
- **Any change to the locked bar** is the owner's, made by formal amendment.

## Noted, not acted on in v2.4

`ctl_instruction.md`'s evaluation section is written as if results exist:
"In our results…" (`:2057`), "In testing our model, we found…" (`:2081`),
"the model passes these tests" (`:2111`). No such results exist. The text is
aspirational, left over from the original write-up, and is not evidence that
the approach works. SCOPE-43 limits v2.4's spec edits to SPEC-07..10, so
FEAS-10's charter records the finding, and the rewrite is routed to the
milestone that sets the bars.
