<derived_ordering_note>
## One derivation the executor must not get backwards (grounded, flagged for review)

**The `stage-{k+1}-init` anchor is saved AFTER the swap, from the NEW controller.**

This is derived, not invented, and the derivation is short: `_save_run_start_anchor`'s docstring (controllers.py:766-777) calls itself "the k=0 analog of the stage-{k+1}-init anchor the ADVANCE branch already saves at every later boundary", and it saves `stage-0-init` from the stage-0 controller BEFORE stage 0 runs. By that stated analogy the init anchor carries the state of the stage about to be ENTERED — so at a k→k+1 boundary it must come from stage k+1's controller.

It is consequential, not cosmetic. `ScheduledWeights.load_state_dict` (scheduled_weights.py:251-262) restores `lambda_phys_max` / `lambda_e_max` / `lambda_l_max` / `n_adam_k` / `warmup_length` FROM THE PAYLOAD. So a stage-k anchor restored during a stage-k+1 RECOVER — a path 40-07 just made live — would overwrite stage k+1's caps with stage k's, reintroducing the exact CR-03 defect this plan exists to close, through the back door.

Correspondingly, `stage-{k}-final` is saved BEFORE the swap, from the OLD controller (it is stage k's terminal state).

Two ACs in Task 1 pin this, and both are needed: the anchor-provenance AC proves the payload's PROVENANCE at save time, and the composed-path AC (W4) forces a real RECOVER after the swap and proves the CONSEQUENCE — that the restore leaves the live schedule on stage 1's caps. Proving only the first would repeat the compositional-coverage mistake that let CR-01 ship (two halves each passing, the joint path never executed).
</derived_ordering_note>
