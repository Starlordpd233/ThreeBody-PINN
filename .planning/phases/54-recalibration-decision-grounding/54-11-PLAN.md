<objective>
Adjudicate the two reports' premise challenges against the packet's own premises,
determine whether the round must be re-framed before its answers are used, and
route to the owner every decision point the evidence surfaced that no source
settles.

Purpose: the runbook's handoff quick reference is unambiguous — before
consolidating or acting, if a verified claim contradicts a package premise, the
round is re-framed before its answers are used, because a false premise
invalidates the answers built on it however well-reasoned they are. Both returned
reports lead with premise-challenge findings, which is what ERW Rule 17 required
of them. Some of those findings, if they hold, change which questions the round
was answering. Determining that is a distinct act from verifying the claims and a
distinct act from merging the menus, and it sits between them.

Output: `54-PREMISE-ADJUDICATION.md` — the adjudication table, the re-frame
determination per round and per surface, and the record of what was routed to the
owner — plus the new gap entries in both registers. This plan disposes of no
decision surface and widens no scope. Where a finding implies either, it says so
and hands it over.

Per D-16 this determination is its own plan ahead of the consolidation; per D-17
a decision point outside the anchor record's scope gets its own identifier with
the granularity choice offered back, which is the D-10 boundary this plan
extends rather than overrides; and per D-09 a ruling is not a gap, so nothing
here is written into the ruling record.
</objective>

either report makes now carries a verdict, and every cross-report agreement
carries an entitlement. This plan is the first in the pass permitted to reason
about what those findings *mean* for the round — and it is permitted to reason
only as far as the boundary SCOPE-34 draws.

**Tracer note.** This phase's tracer is `54-01`. `54-11` is an expansion plan on
that proven shape; no new tracer is emitted.

**Why this is its own plan (D-16).** The runbook places the re-frame check before
consolidation, and the delivery note's join points place the premise merge first,
ahead of any answer being read. Both orderings exist for the same reason: a
consolidation that begins before the premises are settled will have built
structure on a premise that then fails, and the executor discovers it part-way
through. Separating the determination means a contradicted premise halts the pass
cleanly, with nothing to unwind.

**Neutrality standing.** This plan reaches conclusions about *premises* and about
*whether answers are usable*. It reaches no conclusion about what any answer
should be. Those are different acts and the record keeps them apart explicitly.
Bar G is inherited byte-unchanged with its file list extended, as
`54-08-PLAN.md` records.

**Register authority.** This is the first plan of the pass authorized to edit

1. Extract every premise-challenge finding from both reports, using the

   Record the derived total.
2. For each, identify **the packet premise it challenges** and cite it into the
   packet by file and line, resolved by content. Where a challenge names a
   packet item explicitly, use that item; where it does not, locate the premise

3. **Merge duplicates on the premise.** Where both reports challenge the same
   premise, that is one row carrying both challenges, both ledger identifiers and
   both verdicts, plus the entitlement the independence ledger recorded for that
   point. A premise challenged by two agents who could both see the evidence is a
   stronger finding than one challenged by an agent whose class does not admit
   corroboration, and the row records which case it is.
4. **Adjudicate** with a value from a closed set, and no other:
   - `CONTRADICTED` — a verified claim contradicts the premise as the packet
     states it.
   - `QUALIFIED` — the premise holds only under a narrower reading than the
     packet's wording, and the narrower reading is stated.
   - `INTACT` — the challenge does not survive its own verdict, or survives and
     does not reach the premise. State which.
   - `UNSETTLED` — the challenging claim's verdict is `NOT-VERIFIABLE-HERE`, so
     the premise is neither upheld nor contradicted by anything checked. State
     what would settle it and cite the routing the verification report recorded.
   The adjudication follows from the recorded verdict. A row may not be adjudicated
   `INTACT` because the challenge seems unpersuasive, nor `CONTRADICTED` because
   it seems persuasive; the verdict is the evidence and the row cites it.
5. **Identify the answers built on the premise**, by the packet's own question
   numbering, and record the consequence for each with a value from a closed set:
   `USABLE`, `USABLE-QUALIFIED` with the qualification stated, or
   `NOT-USABLE-UNTIL-REFRAMED`. A premise adjudicated `CONTRADICTED` whose answers
   are all recorded `USABLE` is a contradiction in the record and fails the
   acceptance read.
6. **Close the loop.** Enumerate the packet's own stated premises, and record
   which of them no report challenged. An unchallenged premise is recorded as
   unchallenged — not as verified. Both reports were under a duty to challenge
   premises; silence about a premise is weak evidence that it holds and the row
   says so in as many words.

**Where the two reports challenge the same premise but disagree about what
follows**, record both consequences in the row and set the disposition to carry
the disagreement forward rather than resolving it. Averaging is unavailable here
for the same reason it was unavailable in the independence ledger.

**Where a challenge asserts that the packet contradicts itself** — one part of
the packet stating something the packet's own correction record already corrected
elsewhere — that is a package-internal claim, fully settleable, and the row
records both packet locations with their line citations and states which reading
the packet's amendment record makes authoritative. This class does not require
repository access and both reports could reach it, so the entitlement question is
answered by the taxonomy rather than by access.

**Apply the STOP protocol's grounded-versus-silent test to each candidate** and
record the result in the adjudication record before any register is touched. A
candidate is grounded if a source explicitly answers it — the packet, a prior
owner decision, a planning artifact, a framework-spec section revised in an
authorized milestone, or a short uncontroversial first-principles derivation. A
grounded candidate is not a gap and is recorded as grounded with its source. An
ungrounded candidate is classified into one of the protocol's four classes and
only two of them are agent-disposable: a mechanical or presentation-only choice
may be made and noted; an operational-coordination choice may be made if minimal
and factual and **must** be logged. The other two — semantic, and cross-phase
reframing — are surfaced and never invented, as are process-artifact schema
questions.

**Apply step 2's understanding test before writing any gap.** For each candidate
that reaches the surfacing classes, answer the protocol's three questions
explicitly in the adjudication record: whether the principles at stake can be
articulated; whether the different downstream consequences of each candidate

to answer. If any answer is no, **do not write a gap**. Write an understanding
question to the owner instead, in the adjudication record, stating what is not
understood well enough to assess. A gap recorded out of ignorance misleads the
owner and wastes attention, and the protocol says so.
