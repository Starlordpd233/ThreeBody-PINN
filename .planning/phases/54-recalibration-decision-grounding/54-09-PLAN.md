<objective>
Produce the mandatory ERW Rule-19 verification report and fill its first half:
every claim the open-book report grounds in this repository, re-derived
independently, with a verdict.

Purpose: Rule 19 states that a Route-B report's repository claims arrive as
checkable assertions rather than as established facts, that they carry citations
precisely so they can be re-derived, and that the verification is recorded
**before** any claim informs a decision, a roadmap or a spec edit. Everything
downstream in this pass — the premise adjudication, the consolidation, and the
candidate sheet the owner reads at the second checkpoint — consumes claims from
this report. Nothing may consume one before its verdict exists.

Output: the verification report under the reviews tree for this milestone and
phase, carrying the closed verdict vocabulary, the open-book claim ledger with
one row per extracted claim, the re-derivation commands and their outputs, and
the register of claims that cannot be re-derived here together with what would
settle each. The closed-book report's anchors are the next plan's work; this plan
states the boundary and stops at it.

Per D-12 the two verification halves are separate plans writing one file in
sequence, and per D-13 the verdicts are drawn from a closed vocabulary extending
the worked example's own.
</objective>

<planning_provenance>
## Standing

`54-08` established the ground this plan stands on: the response count, the
per-report verification burden derived from the access-mode divergence, the
four-class independence taxonomy, the stamped live-tree pin, and the proof that
the pinned tree and the working tree are identical across every path either
report cites. Without that identity proof a re-derivation performed here would
verify a different file than the one the report read, and would look identical
while doing it.

**Tracer note.** This phase's tracer is `54-01`. `54-09` is an expansion plan on
that proven shape; no new tracer is emitted.

**Why the two verification halves are separate plans (D-12).** The two reports
carry different evidence bases and therefore different verification burdens. The
open-book report's burden is the runbook's own: re-derive each repository-derived
claim. The closed-book report's burden is a provenance triage the runbook does
not describe, because the runbook never anticipated a round whose declared mode
one engagement did not run under. Splitting the passes keeps each burden intact.
They write one file in sequence rather than two files, because the second half's
cross-report reasoning has to sit beside the first half's verdicts to be readable
at all.

**Neutrality standing.** Verifying a claim about a decision surface is not the
same act as disposing of that surface. This plan performs only the first. No
surface is named here by its identifier, no value is attached to any of them, and
the ledger records what a citation says rather than what should be done about it.
Bar G is inherited from `54-06-PLAN.md` byte-unchanged with its file list
extended, as `54-08-PLAN.md` records.

## Deterministic edge probe — the rows this plan discharges

| # | Category | How it binds here |
|---|---|---|
| E2 | region non-emptiness | Every extraction that populates the ledger prints its region size first. An extraction that silently matched nothing would produce an empty ledger that passes a coverage check trivially, which is the exact failure a ledger exists to prevent. The gate therefore requires the derived source count to be greater than zero as well as equal to the ledger count. |
| E3 | duplication | A claim cited in more than one place in the source report is one ledger row, not several. The ledger is keyed on the claim, and every additional citation of the same claim is recorded inside that row. Duplicate rows for one claim would inflate the coverage count and hide a missing claim. |
| E5 | conflict | Where the report's own register row and its body text state the same claim differently, both readings are recorded and the verdict addresses the difference explicitly rather than picking the convenient one. |
| E6 | empty | A verified-and-empty result — a search that legitimately finds nothing, which is itself the evidence — is recorded with an explicit marker rather than as an absent output, because an empty result and a mistyped path look the same. |
| E7 | ordering | Ledger rows are emitted in the source report's own order and every set comparison runs under `LC_ALL=C`. |
</edge_dispositions>

  <action>
For each ledger row whose class admits in-repo re-derivation, do the work in this
order and record each step:

1. **Restate the claim as a checkable assertion** in the row, in the source's own
   terms, so that what is being tested is fixed before the test runs. Rule 19's
   own language is that these claims *arrive* as checkable assertions; making the
   assertion explicit is what turns a citation into something that can fail.
2. **Re-derive it independently.** Read the cited location and its surroundings.
   Do not accept the line number as the evidence — resolve the claim **by
   content**, then record whether the cited line number still resolves to that
   content in the pinned tree. Those are two separate findings and both belong in
   the row: a claim can be true at a line that has moved, and a line number can be
   right while the content does not support the claim.
3. **Record the command and its output.** A command that reads a range, extracts
   a symbol, or counts occurrences. Keep it short enough to paste and specific
   enough to be re-run. Where the evidence is a passage rather than a count,
   quote the passage with its file and its line.
4. **Assign a verdict** from the closed vocabulary and justify it in the note in
   one or two sentences.

**Where a claim rests on a retained run artifact** rather than on source, the
artifact is the primary record and the claim is checked against it directly, not
against a document that summarises it. Record the artifact path, the field or
record read, and the number of records the statistic was computed over — a
statistic computed over a different number of records than the claim assumes is a
distinct finding and gets its own note. Where a claim states a value as
characteristic of a run, record whether the source computed it over the whole
retained record or over a subset, and record which the claim's own wording
implies. A mismatch there is `CONFIRMED-NARROWER`, not `CONFIRMED`.

**Where a claim is a negative** — that something is absent from the tree — the
evidence is a search whose empty result is the finding. Record the search, record
its scope, and print an explicit marker on the empty result, because an empty
result and a mistyped path are indistinguishable in silence. A negative claim
verified by an unscoped search is recorded as `CONFIRMED-NARROWER` with the scope
actually searched stated, never as `CONFIRMED`.

**Where a claim cites a file outside this repository** — a published paper, a
vendored reference implementation's upstream provenance — separate the two parts.
The part that says "this repository contains this text" is re-derivable and is
verified. The part that says "the published source says this" is not, and the row
records the split with a verdict for each part rather than one verdict for both.

**Where re-derivation contradicts the claim**, record `REFUTED` with the evidence
in full, and state what the contradiction does and does not reach. A refuted
claim may sit under a finding whose other assertions survive; the row says which.
Do not soften the wording, do not omit the row, and do not edit the source report.
A refutation is the most valuable output this pass can produce and burying it
would repeat exactly the failure ERW Rule 17 exists to prevent.

**Where the cited location does not carry the attributed content**, record
`UNSUPPORTED` and state where, if anywhere, the content does live. Distinguish
this from `REFUTED` explicitly in the note.

Write the per-claim sections beneath the ledger in the worked example's two-sided
layout — what the specification or the record says, then what the claim says,
then the verdict — for every row whose verdict is anything other than a plain
`CONFIRMED`. Plain confirmations may stay as ledger rows with their command and
output; anything sharper, narrower, refuted or unsupported earns a section,
because those are the rows a later reader will need to reason about.

Record a per-verdict count table at the end of the ledger. The counts must sum to
the number of rows this task was partitioned to handle.
