<objective>
Certify the two returned research reports at intake, record the access-mode
divergence the round did not anticipate, and stamp the live-tree pin without
which no returned citation is re-derivable.

Purpose: ERW Rule 19 makes per-claim in-repo verification a required step before
any returned claim informs a decision, a roadmap, or a spec edit. That
verification is only meaningful against a known tree and a known evidence base.
The round declared a single access mode for every agent; the engagements did not
both run under it. Until that is recorded, the two reports look interchangeable
and a reader would treat agreement between them as confirmation when for one
whole class of claim it is not.

Output: `54-INTAKE.md` — the intake battery, the divergence finding, the
per-report verification burden, and the claim-class independence taxonomy — plus
the stamped live-tree pin in `DELIVERY-NOTE.md`. This plan verifies no claim; it
establishes the ground the two verification plans stand on (D-11 is discharged by
this pass, which is the post-delivery replan that decision deferred).

This plan discharges D-11's deferral and records the new planning
decisions D-12 through D-18 that the delivered evidence made necessary; D-15
freezes the uploaded attachments and D-14 fixes the independence taxonomy every
later plan inherits.
</objective>

## New planning decisions (D-12 … D-18)

`54-CONTEXT.md` carries D-01 … D-11 from the pre-delivery discussion. The
decisions below are new, arise only from evidence that landed after it, and
extend that list without editing it. Each is assembly mechanics — the delegation
of authority recorded in `54-CONTEXT.md` covers how the verification and the
consolidation are structured and verified, and covers no recalibration content.

- **D-12: One verification report file, written across two sequential plans.**
  ERW Rule 19 asks for a verification report, and the worked example the runbook
  cites is a single file. The two returned reports carry substantially different
  evidence bases, so their verification passes are separated into two plans; they
  write the same file in sequence rather than two files, because the second
  pass's cross-report reasoning has to sit beside the first pass's verdicts to be
  readable at all. Sequential ownership of one file is also what keeps the two
  plans in different waves.
- **D-13: The verdict vocabulary is a closed set that extends the precedent's
  own.** The Phase 45 worked example used `CONFIRMED`, a sharpened variant, and a
  `Not verifiable here` section. This pass fixes that as an explicit closed
  vocabulary so no row invents a verdict and so a reader can count verdicts by
  class. Extending a precedent's vocabulary is mechanical; inventing an
  unprecedented adjudication category would not be, and none is introduced.
- **D-14: Agreement counts as corroboration only within a claim class both
  agents could evidence.** Recorded in full in Task 2. This is the direct
  consequence of the Rule-16 divergence and it is the single most consequential
  thing this plan establishes.
- **D-15: Post-upload learning is never written back into an uploaded file.**
  The attachment baseline pin exists so that a reader can reconstruct what the
  external agents actually read. Editing an attachment after upload silently
  destroys that. Everything learned at intake lands in the delivery note, which
  is never uploaded, or in the phase-local intake record.
- **D-16: Premise adjudication is a separate plan ahead of the consolidation.**
  The runbook's handoff quick reference is explicit that a verified claim
  contradicting a package premise means the round is re-framed before its answers
  are used. Putting that determination inside the consolidation plan would have
  an executor discover a fatal contradiction part-way through writing the
  consolidated document.
- **D-17: A new owner-routed decision point outside the anchor gap's scope gets
  its own gap identifier, and the granularity choice is offered back rather than
  assumed.** D-10 chose one anchor gap covering both owner checkpoints rather
  than one record per recalibration sub-question, and `54-CONTEXT.md` Open Item 2
  keeps the alternative available on request. A decision point that is not a
  sub-question of the anchor gap is not covered by that choice, so it is
  recorded separately and the owner is told it can be folded.
- **D-18: The governance record receives pointers, never menus.** Candidate
  menus and marked defaults are the round's contracted output and live in the
  round's consolidated document, attributed to the agent that produced them. The
  ruling record names where they are and what the owner must dispose of. It
  carries no candidate of its own.
