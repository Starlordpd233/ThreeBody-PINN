## First-Principles Physics Check

A deterministic three-body trajectory model conditioned on initial conditions needs a **reconstructive** initial state — i.e., one from which the absolute 3D positions and velocities of all three bodies can be recovered (modulo a fixed frame/gauge convention) — otherwise the conditioning map is non-injective and the network cannot distinguish trajectories that share the conditioning vector but begin in different configurations.

Concretely:

- Invariant-only summaries (Option B's `I₁, I₂, I₃, Q, s_min` + direction cosines) are insufficient. Shape invariants are symmetric under reflections and label permutations; even augmented with two direction cosines, the map from invariants back to absolute body positions has multiple pre-images and loses geometric information needed for absolute prediction.
- Trigonometric angle encodings (Option A) require a body-label-dependent definition of which angle is measured between which bodies' vectors. Once canonical body ordering is fixed (Phase 18 FRAME-01), an angle encoding is no longer needed — the 6D Jacobi velocity vector carries the same orientation information without introducing label-asymmetric trig conventions, and is differentiable everywhere instead of branch-cut-bounded.
- Jacobi coordinates (Option C) provide an exact reduction of the 3-body problem: relative motion separates cleanly from center-of-mass motion, and the two Jacobi position vectors `ρ`, `λ` together with their 3D velocity vectors `ρ̇`, `λ̇` carry the full reconstructive content modulo COM frame and rotation gauge (which Phase 18 FRAME-01 locks). Adding `m₁, m₂, m₃` and `E` closes the dynamical state needed for conditioning.
- Component count: 3 ordered masses + 3 Jacobi position components (after Phase 18 FRAME-01 rotation gauge zeros out `λ_z`) + 6 Jacobi velocity components + 1 total energy = 13 slots, matching `IC₁₃`. Phase 18 FRAME-01 is the locked source of the rotation gauge that reduces 6 position components to the 3 retained slots (`ρ_x`, `λ_x`, `λ_y`).

Tie to D-01 and the verdict `accept-resolution`: only Option C satisfies the reconstructive-injectivity requirement under canonical body ordering without imposing body-label-dependent angle conventions.

## Root-Cause Framework Ambiguity

The pre-Phase-17 framework specs left the IC vector underspecified in two ways:

1. **Slot identity**: `ctl_instruction.md` Step 4 enumerated 9 invariant-derived scalars (`E_norm, L_norm, Q_norm, s_min_norm, I_1_norm, I_2_norm, I_3, I_norm` plus masses) but never named which orientation or velocity slots completed the IC; `pinn_architecture.md` Implementation Blueprint row gestured at "5 physical invariants + 5 trig-encoded angle values" without binding which invariants or which angles.
2. **Total width**: ctl_instruction.md called the input "12D" while pinn_architecture.md called it "14D", with no reconciliation. The IC component count drifted between 11 and 13 across the same document family.

Together these two ambiguities allowed two primary sources to silently disagree on what `IC` even means before a single line of training code could be written.

## Locked Convention

Adopt the Jacobi-state initial-condition vector locked by Phase 17 D-01:

```
IC₁₃ = [m₁, m₂, m₃, ρ_x, λ_x, λ_y, ρ̇_x, ρ̇_y, ρ̇_z, λ̇_x, λ̇_y, λ̇_z, E]
```

The Jacobi position and velocity vectors are defined in scaled coordinates by:

- `ρ = r₂ − r₁`
- `λ = r₃ − (m₁ r₁ + m₂ r₂) / (m₁ + m₂)`
- `ρ̇ = dρ/dt`, `λ̇ = dλ/dt` (full 3D scalars retained)

Position slots `ρ_x`, `λ_x`, `λ_y` are the components retained after the Phase 18 (FRAME-01) rotation gauge. Per-slot normalization is `tanh((z − μ_z) / (σ_z + 10⁻⁸))` with dataset-wide statistics computed after preprocessing. Mass slots are ordered raw masses before tanh normalization (D-07). Energy slot uses `tanh(E / E_scale)` with `E_scale = |median_traj(E)| + 10⁻⁸` (D-06).

The total network input is `x_net = [τ, IC₁₃]` with `τ = 2u − 1`, autograd leaf `u = t/T` (D-03).
